# Ultimate Sales Guide: Show Rate & Connection Rate Mastery
Source: Megalodon Marketing, presented by Jeremy Haynes

Complements `references/sops/follow-up-masterclass.md` (day-by-day follow-up cadence) and `references/sops/objection-handling-masterclass.md` (on-call objection handling). This doc is about the gap in between: getting a ghosting lead to actually respond and show up.

## 1. Core Philosophy: Interest Transfer

- Sales is a game of interest. The salesperson has to hold their own interest at the highest level in order to pull the lead's interest up to match it.
- A lead enters a funnel at "curious," not "convicted." Deals get done at high conviction, on the salesperson's side of the chart, not the lead's. Poor closers give up at the lead's low-interest level instead of raising it, so no deal happens.
- **The tell of a poor closer is the excuse:** "bad lead," "couldn't get a hold of them," "they scheduled and never showed," "they text back then ghost." Every one of these is the same pattern: the lead ghosted, and the rep gave up instead of pivoting how they communicate. Some leads genuinely are unqualified, but assuming that about all of them is a lie reps tell themselves.
- If someone's ghosting you, change the channel or the angle. Don't repeat the same ask.

## 2. Pivot Instead of Giving Up

- Opening text: *"Hey [Name]! [Your name] here with [company]. I just had some time to get back to you about our [product/service] you filled out the form for. Would be great to get on a call to discuss. Would you be open to that?"*
- No response after that plus two calls? Get creative and switch the style of communication entirely rather than repeating the ask.
- **None of this works without real conviction.** If you don't genuinely believe your offer will help them, and you don't actually want their business, there's no deal to be had, it's transparent to the lead either way.

## 3. Selfie Videos

- Quick, personable video texts lift response rate and show rate. Beats a plain text for the same reason a voice note beats a plain text in the follow-up masterclass, harder to ignore a person than a message.
- AI tools like Descript can take one recorded selfie video and swap in each new lead's name automatically, so a single recording scales across a list without sounding like a template.

## 4. Data & Proof Assets

- A real example from the source material: a client managing 500+ Amazon storefronts already generated monthly profit/loss statements for their investors. Compressing hundreds of individual P&Ls into one giant proof PDF and handing it to sales/marketing lifted inbound response rate from 37% to 53% and show rate from 40% to 67%.
- The lesson isn't the specific asset, it's that most reps don't have enough proof material to send, and that's a gap to push back to the business owner/marketer for, not something to work around by talking harder on the call.
- At Icarus, the equivalent asset is the Custom Program Overview video referenced in the follow-up masterclass (not yet built) plus any client results/case-study proof (the public stats already in `context/work.md`: $50M+ revenue generated, 4.8x average ROAS, 95%+ approval rates on restricted verticals).

## 5. Speed and Ownership Beat Automation Alone

- A firsthand story from the source material: shopping for a builder for a 15-acre project, contacted 7 companies. 3 of 7 never followed up at all. 2 of 7 sent only automated reminders. 2 of 7 sent generic automated emails. Zero sent a past-project portfolio, zero sent an FAQ/Q&A resource, and zero had an actual human call or text, despite the buyer answering the qualifying questions about credit score and budget honestly and favorably.
- **The takeaway: automation alone reads as laziness to the buyer**, no matter how qualified the lead is. A human text from a real phone is inherently harder to ignore than an automated one, and that's the rep's edge, not something marketing automation replaces.
- "We're all busy" is not a valid excuse until a rep is genuinely at full capacity from inbound (i.e., has a setter feeding a full calendar). Until then, working leads in the trenches is the job, not something to optimize away.

## 6. Contact Everyone, Not Just "Qualified" Leads

- A client running lead-gen forms assumed people would answer qualifying questions (e.g. "do you have $50k liquid?") honestly. She admitted she'd lie on that question herself even if she qualified. Her team then contacted every lead regardless of stated qualification and outperformed teams in the same niche who only worked "qualified" leads.
- An informal poll of agency owners/marketers backed this up: 74% said they'd answer "no" to a qualifying question even if they had the money.
- **Practical rule: treat every lead as qualified until they show clear, direct signs otherwise.** Filtering out leads based on self-reported qualifying answers alone is filtering out real deals.

## 7. Text-First Selling for Busy, High-Value Buyers

- The source material lists real high-ticket purchases made entirely over text, with no phone call: land parcels, a $35K watch, a 12,000 sq ft rental locked in over FaceTime, luxury vehicles, $15-25K masterminds, designer goods.
- Busier, wealthier buyers default to text and only escalate to a call once they've decided it's worth their time. Pushing every text conversation toward "let's hop on a call" reads as self-serving to this buyer type, not helpful.
- The bias flips for less qualified or older buyers, they lean toward phone calls by default. Read the buyer, don't apply one channel preference to everyone.

## 8. Leverage Social Media Lookups

- Get the lead's first and last name and look them up on Instagram. Establishing contact outside of text/email alone reportedly makes up to a 30% difference in reaching otherwise-unresponsive leads.
- Keep your own profile recognizably tied to the business you're representing, so a lead who looks you up can confirm you're a real person from a real company, not a cold stranger.

## 9. The "We're All Busy" Selfie Hook

- Used specifically on a lead who showed real interest on a call, then went quiet during follow-up. A short selfie video opening with some version of "hey, I know we're all busy" reportedly gets responses reliably in that exact situation, interested-then-ghosted, not cold leads.

## 10. The "Red Flags" Follow-Up

Use when a lead showed real interest, then went dark. Works as a pattern interrupt precisely because it's a little confrontational, it invites the real objection out instead of another vague "just checking in."

> "Lmk, scares me when potential clients aren't responsive to simple questions and comes off as red flag. Our best deals respond, are transparent with us, and get us what we need when we ask. If we want to do a few million a month together I need you onboard fully."

In the source example, a lead who'd gone silent for about 2 weeks responded to this with a real explanation (needed 2 more weeks to launch) and closed 2 weeks later. The line works because it names the actual dynamic instead of pretending nothing's wrong.

## 11. Persistent, Not Pushy: Value-Driven vs. Selfish Follow-Up

- Follow up at reasonable intervals (every few days), rotating between different value-driven angles so it never reads as the same nudge twice. This is the same "never repeat the message" rule as the follow-up masterclass's Days 2-3 and cold-reactivation cadence.
- **Value-driven example:** *"Hey [Name]! I made a [doc/resource] on how a [their type of business] is [specific outcome] using [specific method]. Mind if I share it plus an example?"* Leads with something useful, asks permission before sending it.
- **Selfish follow-up (avoid repeating these with no new value attached):**
  - "Following up on my last message"
  - "Following up"
  - "Want to jump on a quick call together?"
  - "I have 15 min free if you're available?"
  - "Haven't heard from you, are you still interested?"
- Most reps give up not because the lead is truly gone, but because they run out of creative, valuable follow-up angles and default to one of the selfish lines above, which reads as one-sided and gets ignored.

## 12. Low Morale Kills Deals

- Morale is treated as a real input to close rate, not just a feeling. The moment a rep stops believing they'll close a given batch of leads, that belief becomes self-fulfilling, same as a team that thinks it's losing starts playing worse.
- If a rep genuinely can't stay convicted on a set of leads or a strategy, the source material's advice is blunt: say so and get moved off it, rather than keep working leads half-heartedly. Working leads you don't believe in wastes the business's money, not just the rep's time.

## 13. Lead With Value, Not a Pitch

Most outreach falls into one of two weak patterns:
1. **Random rapport opener** ("hey how's it going, how long have you been running your business?") with no direction.
2. **Straight pitch** ("we'll get you [outcome] in [timeframe], interested?") with no earned trust yet.

**Instead:** lead with value, then ask permission to explain further. Don't ask "do you want X outcome" outright, ask if you can educate them on the specific process that gets customers to X outcome. Subtle shift, much higher response.

## 14. Micro-Commitments Before the Booking Link

- A lead agreeing to "learn more" doesn't mean they'll actually book. There's a last moment of hesitancy right before committing to a calendar link.
- Get one more small "yes" immediately before sending the link, rather than sending it the instant they show interest: *"Awesome, I'll send the link to my calendar then, ok?"* Each prior "yes" in the conversation makes the next one more likely, so cash in one more before the actual ask.

## 15. How to Use This SOP (Claude Code)

- Use this alongside `follow-up-masterclass.md` when a lead has gone quiet after showing real interest (not cold pipeline, that's the masterclass's cold-reactivation cadence). This doc's tools (red flags follow-up, "we're all busy" selfie hook, value-first re-engagement) are specifically for the "showed interest, then ghosted" case.
- When drafting a value-driven follow-up, it needs an actual resource or insight attached, not just a soft nudge. If there's nothing genuinely new to offer, don't send one of the selfish-follow-up patterns instead, flag to Bryce that a real asset is needed rather than manufacturing a fake one.
- The "red flags" text is a pattern-interrupt tool, not a default. Use it specifically for leads who went dark after showing real interest, not as a first touch or on cold leads.
- Selfie video scripts can be drafted as copy (what to say), same as the voice note scripts in the follow-up masterclass, actual recording and sending stays with Bryce.

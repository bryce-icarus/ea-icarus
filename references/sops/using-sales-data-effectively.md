# How to Use Sales Data Effectively
Source: Sales Sniper SOP

## Core Principles

- **Minimizing variables** — identify and address the smallest, most insignificant part of a process that may be contributing to an issue. Isolating variables avoids unnecessary changes and helps pinpoint true root causes.
- **Sequential changes** — make one change at a time and give it appropriate time to show impact before making the next. Avoids confusion from stacking multiple simultaneous changes.
- **Distinguishing skills vs. processes** — processes are easier and faster to change; skill development takes time and varies by individual. Don't treat a process fix and a skill fix the same way.
- **Root cause analysis** — find the actual root cause before implementing changes. Fixing symptoms instead of causes leads to ineffective solutions.
- **Externalities** — the 4th of July and Christmas Day will almost always suck for sales. Don't panic or overreact to dips caused by known external events.
  - *Process optimization with externalities:* instead of reacting immediately, adapt strategy/process to accommodate the known external factor (plan for it rather than "fix" it).
  - *Sequential monitoring with externalities:* keep watching through the externality period specifically, and assess whether your adaptations are mitigating the negative effect or capturing upside.
- **Data-driven decision-making** — use data and evidence to decide which changes to make and how to prioritize them, not gut feel alone.
- **Process optimization** — apply incremental, data-driven changes and keep monitoring results systematically to converge on an optimal process.

## Worked Example: High Cart Abandonment Rate

1. **Minimizing variables** — don't overhaul the whole site; investigate to isolate the specific contributing factors.
2. **Sequential changes** — narrow to one factor first (e.g. the checkout process) rather than changing everything at once.
3. **Skills vs. processes** — this is a process problem (checkout flow), not a user-skill problem.
4. **Root cause analysis** — dig into checkout and find that payment-step page load times are excessively long — likely root cause.
5. **Externalities** — account for known bad periods (e.g. holidays) separately so they don't get misread as caused by the process issue.
6. **Data-driven decision-making** — confirm via analytics and user feedback that load time is a real, significant issue before acting.
7. **Process optimization** — prioritize fixing payment-page load time specifically (server performance, code optimization) rather than a broader redesign.
8. **Sequential monitoring** — track cart abandonment after the fix; a steady decline over following weeks confirms the change worked.

**Takeaway:** isolate the smallest likely-relevant variable, change one thing, measure with real data, and don't let known external noise (holidays, etc.) distort your read on what's working.

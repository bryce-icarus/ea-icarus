# Objection Handling Masterclass

## The Most Important Concept: Knowing How to Read People

Three levels of sales competency:
1. **Knowing what to say** — the questions to ask, and the order.
2. **Understanding why you're asking** — what response you're trying to elicit.
3. **Knowing what you're hearing** — listening past the words to the conversation happening in the prospect's head. This is the skill that lets you have the real conversation instead of one filled with fluff.

Level 3 is the target. Prospects give fluff after price is on the table, say they'll decide Friday and ghost — reading between the lines is what prevents that. This comes from time and reps: the more conversations, situations, tonalities, and energy patterns you encounter, the faster you can smell true vs. false.

## Objection Handling Framework

**Diffuse → Tie Down → Clarify → Reframe → Ask**

### Step 1 — Diffuse
"No worries / not a problem / no stress / totally cool."

### Step 2 — Tie Down
Confirms the prospect is bought into the *process* before moving into finance/partner objections, and surfaces whether this is the real objection.

- "Before you go away and speak with the team, in your opinion, do you feel like what we went through today can be the answer for you?" *(assess body language and tonality)*
- "What specifically makes you feel like it can?"

Getting them to say yes gives you "permission" to objection-handle — this is your anchor.

### Step 3 — Clarify
The most important step, and where most time is spent. You can't handle what you don't understand — seek to understand (STU) first. Often, once the situation is fully clear, the next step is just logical ("if A is true and B is true, wouldn't it make sense to get started?") and no fancy reframe is needed.

- "So when you go away and speak with the team, what specifically are you wanting to run by them? Just to see if I can help."
- "In the past, with decisions like this, what might've come up and prevented you from moving forward?"
- "How do you think they would respond? What would they be most opposed to?"

### Step 4 — Reframe
Ask permission before offering a perspective (mirrors the "anchor" from Tie Down):
- "Can I offer a different perspective?"
- "Can I share another perspective?"
- "Can I offer a potential solution for that?"
- "Can I share what a lot of other clients are doing right now for that?"

Then offer a logical solution to the stated objection (timing, money, etc.) — no need for mental gymnastics or elaborate analogies. If they refuse a logical solution, the stated objection wasn't the real one; the real objection is uncertainty (see below).

Example (timing objection):
> "What we generally do in situations like this is we can get everything set up, get you in the system, learning the process BEFORE you start taking meetings — that way after the new year you're prepared to take full advantage of the appointments. I actually had a guy that joined in March and didn't start taking meetings until July, and he closed the first 3 because he spent those first couple months getting ready. Does that sound like something you'd want to take advantage of?"

### Step 5 — Ask
Just ask — confidently. There has to be a leader in the closer/prospect relationship; it needs to be you.

Retracing the prospect's own words removes their ability to dispute the logic:
> "Ok so you feel this would help, you want to go and speak to the team which I respect, and you think they would be in support of this as well… so would it make logical sense to just get started now?"

Direct asks: "Ready to get started?" / "Ready to rock and roll?" / "Can I welcome you to the family?"

## Handling Smokescreens: "I Need to Think About It"

Not a real objection — it's a mask. Job is to get to the truth underneath it.

1. "Not a problem. Real quick question before you think it over — does the program make sense and can you see a return on investment?" *(if uncertainty: "Why do you see the return specifically?")*
2. "And just so I understand — what are the thoughts or hesitations that are coming up in your mind that's making you want to go away and think about it? Just to see if I can help."
   - If money comes up → move to Handling Money.
   - If not: "Is it the money, the timing, are you a little uncertain about it, like what is it that's giving you that pause? Just to see if I can help with anything while you've still got me." Use a light, disarming line if needed ("you probably didn't think too long about what to have for lunch").

By this point you should have the real objection identified.

## Handling Uncertainty

The core objection underneath most stalls. Non-uncertainty objections are logistical (timing, money) and are usually smokescreens that are easy to overcome logically — flush those out first.

### The 4 types of fear/uncertainty
1. **Fear of you** — the company won't deliver (a trust objection).
2. **Fear of me** — they themselves won't be able to hold up their end and get results.
3. **Fear of the future** — fear of failure/the unknown/doing something new.
4. **Fear of the past** — they were burned before and fear repeat.

Uncertainty usually surfaces as #1 or #2, but is *caused* by #3 or #4. Fears 1+4 tend to pair, and 2+3 tend to pair. Identify both what they're afraid of and where it's coming from.

This is an **upstream issue** — ideally identified in discovery and handled in the pitch. Much harder to fix at the end of the call.

### Getting mutual buy-in that it's uncertainty
- "Would it be fair to say you're looking for a bit of certainty?"
- "Sounds like you're just not 100% sure if this is going to work for you?"

### Splitting fear of you vs. fear of me
- "Are you mostly uncertain about our ability to deliver? Or you're confident we'll deliver, you're just a bit unsure about your own ability to close the appointments? Where's your head at?"

Get a real answer — if they're being polite/vague, joke it out: "Well there must be something because otherwise we'd be getting set up." / "You won't hurt my feelings if it's us, just shoot me straight."

### Handling Fear of Me (can't execute)
- "Where do you think that concern comes from?"
- Build them up: reframe trust-building as a transferable skill they already have, just applied to a new channel.
- "If you were in a community of 400+ advisors who had the same concern and overcame it, do you feel like there's any reason you wouldn't be able to do the same — assuming you followed the same process?"
- "So assuming you came in, WOULD you follow that process?"
- "So if you think the process works, and you're committed to following it… what's left?"
- "Ready to get started?"
- Bake in a relevant testimonial (someone who started skeptical/unskilled and still won).

### Handling Fear of You (won't deliver)
- Get clarity on exactly what they doubt: getting appointments in the first place, or quality of the appointments?
- If quality — clarify further: intent level, asset level, or something else specifically?
- If it's volume: conviction your way through with real numbers (years in business, ad spend, appointments generated).
- If it's quality: address the specific filter/criteria that ensures it (e.g. a minimum asset threshold, screenshots/proof channels).

Clarity is everything — you can't hit a target in the dark, and guessing at the objection erodes trust and wastes the call.

## Handling Money

**Diffuse:** "Not a problem, totally get it."

**Tie Down:** "Money aside, do you feel like this could be the answer for you?" → "What makes you feel like it can?" (get their internal justification).

**Fork — fear vs. logistics:**
"In terms of the money, is it a case that you just don't have the full amount, or is it a case that the money is there, you're just not sure if you want to spend it here?"

**If they have the money:**
"Ok so the money is there. Would it be fair to say you're looking for a bit of certainty here?" → move into Handling Uncertainty.

**If they don't have the full amount:**
"Ok, that's not a problem — if you did have, say, a duffle bag of $17k, would you do it?"
- If yes: "So if there was a way we could break it up, make it more manageable, would that help you get started?" → walk through payment plans if yes; move to Uncertainty if no.

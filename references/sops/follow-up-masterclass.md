# Follow-Up Masterclass SOP
### Source: Evan Brown, B2B Sales Coaching Call (June 24)
### Purpose: Reference doc for Claude Code. Use this as the authoritative framework when building follow-up sequences, writing follow-up copy, drafting voice note scripts, building CRM automations, or generating end-of-month campaigns.

---

## 1. Core Philosophy

- **The gap between a $15-20K/mo closer and a $40-60K/mo closer is NOT one-call-close rate.** Most closers at the same company have roughly the same number of one-call closes. The difference is pipeline squeezing: inputs and follow-up volume.
- **People buy conviction.** Follow-up will never uncover deep new pain or emotion over text. The ONLY thing that pierces through in follow-up is how convicted the message sounds. Every follow-up asset (email, text, voice note) must be conviction-based, not "just checking in."
- **The stat is real: most deals close on the 8th-12th touchpoint.** Some prospects reach touchpoint 8 through pre-call material and close on call one. Most don't, which is why one-call-close rates sit at 30-40%.
- **Deals can take a very long time.** Example from the call: a $35K deal closing 18 months after the first call (Jan 2025 to mid 2026). Another prospect: 39 texts and ~50 emails before booking. Never let leads go.
- **Volume standard:** 20-30 follow-ups per day minimum across email, text, phone, and voice note. Compounded daily, this is worth an extra $5-10K/month minimum.
- **Lazy follow-up gets ignored.** "Hey, just checking in" copy signals a lazy salesperson and gets zero response. Expert-level copy plus a conviction voice note roughly triples the likelihood the prospect shows to the follow-up call.
- **Differentiation principle:** almost no one sends conviction voice notes. Being different is itself the edge.
- **A lead is only dead if they explicitly say "don't contact me again."** Everything else stays in rotation (cadence slows to once every 2-3 months for cold ones, but never stops). Compliance note: honor SMS opt-outs strictly. If someone texts STOP, stop texting; email remains a valid channel if they're still on the list.
- **Send to EVERYONE in the pipeline.** Not just "good" prospects. Even prospects you pushed too hard, burned, or who ghosted. Persistence itself builds credibility ("you've followed up for two years, clearly you have something going on"). The worst outcome is no response.

## 2. The Day-One Follow-Up Sequence (After Every Single Call)

Every non-close call gets ALL THREE channels the same day. Non-negotiable. 8 calls = 8 full sequences.

### Step 1: Recap Email (within 5 minutes of the call, 60 minutes max)
- **Subject:** "Recap from our call" (or "Recap: what [outcome] looks like for you")
- **Body structure:**
  1. Warm open: enjoyed the conversation, acknowledge where they're at.
  2. Quick recap of what was discussed.
  3. Embed/link the **Custom Program Overview video** (see Section 3).
  4. Confirm the scheduled follow-up call date/time.
  5. Conviction close: direct statement that this will work for them.
- Sent after EVERY call, EVERY day. No exceptions.

### Step 2: Personal Phone Text (same day)
- Sent from your **personal phone** (green/blue bubble, real number, NOT the CRM number). This builds trust; personal-number texts noticeably outperform CRM texts.
- First text: "Hey, this is [Name] on my personal phone, just wanted you to have my number."
- The tie-down for this happens ON the call (see Section 4), so they're expecting it and have already saved your contact.

### Step 3: Conviction Voice Note (end of every day, before you're allowed to end your day)
- Sent right after the personal text, to every call taken that day. ~30 minutes total per day.
- **This is the single highest-leverage asset in the sequence.** Treat it as a personal daily standard: no gym, no dinner, no ending the day until the voice notes are sent.
- **Voice note script framework (adapt, don't read verbatim):**
  > "Hey [Name], I know we talked at [time] and we've got our follow-up set for [day/time], do me a favor and accept that calendar invite. One more thing I wanted to get across because I know we had a good conversation and I know you're serious about [their goal]. Between you and me, I have 100% conviction that we can absolutely make [offer/outcome] work for you. Take it with a grain of salt, but I know that if you make this jump, as uneasy and uncomfortable as it is, this will be one of the best decisions you make all year, bar none. I'm excited to talk on [day]. If you have any questions at all, seriously, let me know. I know for a fact we can [change outcome], and I'm excited to make it happen."
- **Rules for the voice note:**
  - No selling. No "here's why we're the best." No feature talk.
  - Pure conviction plus light reference to their situation.
  - Voice note beats selfie video for B2B (higher open rate, conviction lands better, less corny). Selfie video is acceptable if it works for you; the medium matters less than the felt conviction.
  - Works to Android too (arrives as an audio file).

### Result of Day One
The prospect now has three associations with you across three formats: written (email), video (program overview), and audio (voice note), all conviction-based.

## 3. The Custom Program Overview Video (Pre-Call AND Post-Call Asset)

- A 4-5 minute Loom-style video. Essentially half your pitch plus context to sell.
- **Answers what B2B buyers always want:** what you do, how it works, how it helps them, what outcome they get. (Price excluded.)
- **Send it twice:** as pre-call material AND in the post-call recap email.
- **What it does:** neutralizes the A-type "what do you do, tell me how it works" opener. Prospect arrives (or returns) with a working understanding of the offer, so calls start further down the field.
- A great one gets rewatched and shared; Evan's has 1,000+ views like a YouTube video.
- **Build spec:** clear, direct, outcome-focused, high-level on process (details live on the call).
- **Status for Icarus:** does not exist yet. Flagged as a to-build item, see the current-priorities/backlog note. Until it exists, the recap email template leaves a placeholder line rather than a broken link.

## 4. On-Call Tie-Downs (Set Up the Follow-Up Before Hanging Up)

At the end of every non-close call (with a follow-up booked):

**Phone number capture and text tie-down:**
> "Awesome, we have a call for Friday at 3. Let me just do this real quick, what's your cell? Cool, I just sent you a text. Do me a huge favor: save my contact and text back so I know you've got it. I'll also shoot over an email recap, everything we talked about today in video and written format so you're most prepared for Friday. Is that something you could look at before the call? Awesome, and we're good for Friday at 3?"

**Show-rate direct tie-down (Evan's style, use for lukewarm prospects):**
> "Can I say something real quick before we run? I speak with six [prospect archetype] a day, and a lot of times we end up booking a meeting just to book a meeting. You don't strike me as the type to book it and not show. Shoot me straight, is this something you're actually serious about, or is there something else going on? ... Cool, so we're good for Friday, you're not going to leave me out in the dust?"

**Show-rate management rules:**
- Confirm text: night before (for AM calls) or morning of (for PM calls).
- If ~1 hour out with no text reply, no email reply, and no video view, **mark the slot free** and let a fresh lead take it.
- **Read the prospect to decide how hard to protect the slot:**
  - "Yeah... maybe in a week or so, I need to sort out money": triple tie-down on the call; mark free aggressively if no confirmation.
  - "Dude, I love this, give me 48 hours, let's talk Thursday": protect the slot; high show probability.

**Call outcome distribution benchmark:** on 10 calls, expect ~1-2 one-call closes and ~5-6 booked follow-ups.

**How a no-show actually gets detected at Icarus:** `daily-plan` doesn't take Bryce's word for who showed up, it checks whether a Gemini notes / read.ai transcript email arrived for each scheduled call (see Section 5's automation note and the `daily-plan` skill). A scheduled call with no transcript by the next check is flagged as a likely no-show, never assumed automatically, Bryce confirms before anything gets logged in Close or enrolled in the re-engagement workflow.

## 5. Extended Cadence and Automation

- **Days 1-3: fully manual** (email, personal text, voice note). Manual = personal = trust. The source material only scripts Day 1 in full (the recap/text/voice-note triad, Section 2) and what happens from Day 4+; it doesn't specify Day 2 or Day 3 content. At Icarus, the `lead-follow-up` skill fills that gap with a light personal-text touch each of those two days, a different angle each time, tracked so it doesn't get lost on a heavy call day. See the skill for the exact mechanism.
- **Day 4+: automated email sequence in the CRM.** Evan uses GoHighLevel; Icarus runs Close.io. Built as a real Close.io workflow, **`Icarus Follow-Up: Day 4+ Sequence`** (`seq_5ihhG5wGdldYXDVSjbbEHR`), 4 email steps landing ~day 4/8/14/21 after enrollment, each a distinct angle (conviction, proof/stats, objection pre-empt, close-the-loop). Trigger is `lead-manual`, meaning it only fires when Bryce manually enrolls a specific lead in Close, nothing auto-enrolls. Created in Draft status; must be switched to Active in Close before it will actually send. See the `lead-follow-up` skill for how this fits into the daily workflow.
- **No-show re-engagement, a separate case.** A lead who booked a discovery call and never showed isn't the same as one who took the call and went quiet, different tone, faster cadence. Built as its own Close.io workflow, **`Icarus No-Show Re-Engagement (Auto)`** (`seq_5SUflaFnSekcrpnNBYyyvK`), 3 email steps at ~1hr/day3/day7 after enrollment, each linking Bryce's real scheduling link to rebook. Still Draft status until Bryce activates it, but the trigger is `lead-event` filtered to the Close lead status **No Show**, meaning setting a lead's status to No Show in Close is itself the enrollment, no separate manual-enroll step (Bryce's choice, replacing an earlier `lead-manual` version). Only fires after Bryce confirms it's really a no-show (see below), that confirmation now doubles as authorizing the sequence, not just a status label.
- **Texts are NEVER automated.** All texts stay on the personal phone.
- **Ghosted does not mean dead.** Re-engage with a changed offer: split-pay option, adjusted terms, pattern-interrupt humor ("you're harder to get in front of than my ex-girlfriend"). Vary the angle every touch.
- **Cold/ghosted long-term cadence:** email every 2-3 months with fresh, good copy until they buy or opt out entirely. It might take 29 or 37 touchpoints.
- **Micro-habit system:** replace scroll time with follow-up reps, send texts/voice notes while eating, walking, during no-shows, between calls. Build the dopamine association: 100 texts leads to ~3 responses leads to 2 calls leads to 1 close. Do that twice a month = 2 extra closes/month.
- **No-show time is follow-up time.** Pipeline outreach is higher leverage than calling a no-show, because it's a direct offer to someone who already wants the thing.

## 6. Pipeline Infrastructure

- Every lead auto-flows into a pipeline tracker (Evan uses Zapier to spreadsheet/CRM): stages for **Warm, objection type, Deposit, Closed**, with a "hot list" section. At Icarus this maps to Close.io lead statuses and pipelines.
- Track date of last contact per lead. Evan's list: ~735 people, of which ~550 below the hot-list line got the end-of-month blast.
- Most closers have **hundreds of thousands of dollars sitting in pipeline** they never touch. The list IS the asset.
- **Setter relationship is pipeline infrastructure:** tip/gift your setter periodically (Evan sends ~$250 every month or two, no strings). Result: the setter routes you the revived deposit-ghost leads and highest-intent bookings. Also: share your winning email templates with your setter, when they make money from your system, you get better leads. (Icarus note: Bryce is currently the only person on the sales team, so this applies if/when a setter role exists.)
- If you don't have time to send 500 blast emails, give your setter email access and have THEM rip the sends (they earn commission on resulting closes; you earn the same per deal).

## 7. The End-of-Month Squeeze (The $50-100K/Quarter Play)

Sent to the ENTIRE pipeline (hundreds of people) in the last week of the month / end of quarter. Done consistently, monthly or at minimum every quarter.

### Anatomy of the squeeze email
1. **It must be a RE-OFFER, not a check-in.** Something genuinely new: a new guarantee program, zero-risk structure, limited pricing, new terms. ("We rolled out a zero-risk program we've never offered before that guarantees X.")
2. **8-Mile the resistance** (pre-empt the objection they're already thinking):
   > "I know this sounds ridiculous and you've been promised the moon and stars before, but I stake my reputation on this being completely different than anything you've seen."
3. **The Sales Competition Frame** (the conversion engine):
   > "Transparently, I'm a few enrollments away from setting a company record, which would do a lot for me personally. Because of that, I'm incentivized to give you the best deal possible. I'd be able to pull a couple strings and get you in for much less up front."
4. **Direct booking link** (Calendly hooked directly into the email) so hot responses self-book. These booked calls often close as near-lay-downs.
5. Split-pay offers are fine here, half now beats zero.

### Sales Competition Frame usage rules
- Reusable indefinitely, even on the same leads. If called out: "Yeah, I broke my record in January, records move, goalposts change, but this one matters more." Total detachment; you cannot care.
- Best deployed at end of quarter (especially when selling to salespeople, they intuitively get quota pushes). Rotate other squeeze angles mid-quarter.
- Works at the start of a month too: "I know it's the 4th, I'm on pace and I need you. Honestly, I need you more than you need me."
- Make it personal: "Don't do this for you, do it for me." Conviction plus a personal stake gets people over the line who've stalled for months.

### The Third-Party / Manager Check-In Email (advanced squeeze variant)
Sent from a different sender (assistant email, "head of enrollment," a manager persona):
> "Hey [Name], this is [Manager]. I saw you had a strategy call with my right-hand man [Closer], but it didn't move forward. I wanted to check how the conversation went, did he show up on time? Anything he could have done better? For context, you're someone we can definitely help, and we're running a special where I can get you in for less than half the original cost. What was the main hesitation?"
- Optional spice: "he's been on edge lately" / "he's on the chopping block."
- Surfaces prospects who liked the program but not the rep, and re-opens dead threads at a shockingly high response rate.
- Use once a month to once a quarter. Keep it compliant (fresh sender does not mean it's ok to violate an SMS opt-out).

### Expected math
500 sends won't feel fun. But 500 sends leads to ~10 fresh bookings leads to ~5 closes. That's the $50-100K/quarter delta. It will not happen in 5 texts, 5 emails, or 50 calls.

## 8. Split-Pay to Paid-In-Full (PIF) Conversion Script

Use on EVERY split-pay close:
1. Prospect agrees to split pay (e.g., $5K + $5K on a $10K program). **Get the card. Do not charge it yet.**
2. Have a second checkout link pre-built: PIF with a real incentive (e.g., $500-$1,000 off, so $9,500).
3. Start onboarding, then:
   > "Can I throw something at you without offending you? Here at the end of the month I'm extremely close to breaking our company sales record. You strike me as a guy who'd rather not spend more than he has to, so I figured you'd be mad if I didn't tell you: if we could save you $1,000 AND help me hit the record by taking care of the whole payment today, is that something you'd be completely against?"
4. On verbal yes, copy the card info into the PIF link.
- Conversion rate on this ask is very high (~7 of 10 in Evan's experience). Seven PIFs instead of seven splits.
- The frame that makes it work: name the salesy-ness before they can ("I know I'm being the salesy guy, but I figured you'd be mad if I didn't tell you").

## 9. Objection Handling vs. Follow-Up Bucket (When to Stop Pushing on the Call)

- **Push harder when they're future-pacing:** "If we worked together, what would that look like? How would this integrate?" Buying-type questions = license to push.
- **Ease off into the follow-up bucket when:** they only talk about themselves, their objections, their circumstances, no future-pacing.
- The calculus: pushing a resistant prospect might close 30% now; giving room plus running this follow-up system closes ~70% later. Take the odds.
- Archetype-adjust the line: analytical buyer, A-type, has-a-partner, single-mom-decision-maker all have different tolerance. "Play the player."
- The only way to truly find the line is to occasionally cross it, and burned prospects still belong in the follow-up list. Burn on the call is recoverable through consistent conviction follow-up ("Honestly I didn't like you, but clearly you have something going on, let's talk").

## 10. Daily and Monthly Standards Checklist

**Daily (non-negotiable):**
- [ ] Recap email within 5-60 min of every call (with program overview video)
- [ ] Personal-phone intro text same day for every new call
- [ ] Conviction voice note to every call taken today (before ending the day)
- [ ] 20-30 total follow-up touches across email/text/phone/voice note
- [ ] Confirmation texts for tomorrow's follow-up calls
- [ ] No-show slots become pipeline outreach time

**Weekly/Monthly:**
- [ ] Every follow-up call booked before hanging up (target: 5-6 follow-ups booked per 10 calls)
- [ ] Ghosted leads re-engaged with a changed angle (not the same message twice)
- [ ] End-of-month squeeze blast to full pipeline (re-offer plus sales competition frame)
- [ ] Quarterly: third-party/manager check-in variant
- [ ] Setter relationship maintained (tip/gift plus share winning templates), n/a while Bryce is solo on sales
- [ ] Personal monthly cash standard set, and a self-bet/stake if possible to force intensity

## 11. Instructions for Claude Code (How to Use This Doc)

When generating assets from this framework:
1. **Contextualize everything to the specific offer.** Pull the lead's vertical, price point, and objections from Close.io before adjusting any template. Change messaging only slightly; the structure and conviction tone are the system.
2. **Every asset must pass the conviction test:** no "just checking in," no neutral language, no corporate softness. Direct, certain, personal.
3. **Preserve the multi-channel structure:** email (written) plus video (program overview, once it exists) plus voice note script (audio) for day one; automated email only from day 4+.
4. **Re-offers must contain something genuinely new** (guarantee, terms, pricing, structure), never a repackaged check-in.
5. **Respect compliance:** SMS opt-outs are permanent for texting; email cadence for cold leads is every 2-3 months.
6. **Automation status at Icarus:** Days 1-3 stay draft-and-review, Claude drafts the recap email (Gmail draft, not sent), writes the personal text and voice note script, and creates a Close.io task for Bryce to send the text and record the voice note. Bryce reviews and sends everything himself. Day 4+ has a real, built Close.io workflow (`Icarus Follow-Up: Day 4+ Sequence`, `seq_5ihhG5wGdldYXDVSjbbEHR`), but it sits in Draft status and only fires on manual per-lead enrollment, so leads only enter it when Bryce deliberately puts them there. Do not switch it to Active, change its trigger to an automatic one, or build additional live-sending workflows without checking with Bryce first, since that's a shared-system change that touches real leads.
7. **Copy quality bar:** expert-level, pattern-interrupting, personality allowed (humor, directness). Lazy copy is worse than no send.
